## About

The IEEE University of Zambia Student Branch in the year 2025 set a bold and audacious goal, to executive a service-learning, 
community project. We applied to the EPICS  in IEEE program (EPICS - Engineering Projects in Community Service), the motivation 
behind this was sparked after I had read the IEEE impact report. Rather than reading about the amazing stories from IEEE members 
(the largest technical organisation in the world), I chose to be part of that change and spearheaded a call to action. Though passing 
round one and ultimately not making it passed round two, I learn a lot during the process.

---

## Problem Statement

Kanakantapa Primary School, a school located in Chisamba in the Central Province, Zambia, was
established in 1982. Since then, they have had no access to electricity. In 2022, four desktop computers and a
generator were donated by the Zambian government to the school. Unfortunately, this was not sustainable,
and as a result, they were not able to conduct computer studies for the pupils.
Additionally, the borehole at the school is damaged and outputs muddy water. This poses a critical problem
among pupils in the surrounding community because they are prone to waterborne diseases.

---

## Project Description

For the project, PowerKPS (short for Power Kanakantapa Primary School), we propose the installation of
a solar PV system to power the computer lab of the primary school, so as to allow access to quality education
and computer literacy skills to the primary school pupils. Additional lighting of the rest of the rooms and
finally the establishment of a new water pump that can provide access clean water for the pupils.

---

## Reflection

This experience taught me to improve on the ability to explain technical concepts to a non-technical audience. 
Additionally, the amount of funds we were bidding ($10,000) could not accommodate the scope of our project. 
We would have had to be spilt in into two. Namely, separate funding for the borehole and the computer lab respectively. 
Lastly, we needed to strengthen our community partnership with non-profit organisations. Though we managed to strike a 
partnership between the student team and the Zambia Renewable Energy Association (ZARENA), we needed more allies to support our cause.
